# Leading in a way you will be followed
## Background
At time of writing, I am not formally in a position of leadership, though in my previous jobs, I've made my way to leadership positoins relatively quickly. 
I can't say for certain why I generally get promoted to team lead or manager quickly, but I follow the things I'm saying in the remainder of this article more-or-less at all times.
I haven't cemented my position as an engineering manager yet for a number of reasons (the following is all speculation)
1. I'm generally too young to be taken seriously as a technical architect/team lead/manager 
  - I am 28 at the time of writing and most team leads/managers at large organizations are generally older and if they're younger, they will have been at the company for most of their career
2. I haven't been able to stay at a job long enough to cement myself
  - My first team lead position was not a software development one, but at a tech support help desk run by my college. I was a student team leader, and I graduated and was thrust into the professional world
  - I worked for a startup, and 4 months after being made leader of the development team for a project, the entire company ran out of money and laid us all off
  - I got a job at a megacorp, and after leading a team successfully for a year, the entire department (including many levels above me) was laid off due to internal politics.

All my excuses above withstanding, I believe I have something interesting to say, so I'll say it anyway

## Philosophy
Leadership is generally simply described. It's working with others to accomplish a goal. 
This definition is broad, vague, and does not include a number of aspects that many insert out of their own prejudice. 
1. Leadership is not reserved for people formally declared to be leaders
2. Leadership cannot be done without a goal
3. Leadership is not constrained to an occupational task
4. Leadership does not mean control
5. Leadership does not mean coersion
6. Leadership is not working together to accomplish the formal leader's goal

The three levels of leading people in order of effectiveness:
1. Respect
A respected leader is an effective leader. Once respect is earned, it is difficult for people NOT to join with the respected and work together to accompllish goals. Respect is also a two way street, (a truism, I know) requiring the leader to show respect to the non-leaders if the leader wants to lead through this avenue
3. Fear
A feared leader accomplishes goals and complete tasks via fear. The fear may be derived through shame, physical consequences, or (most likely) the implicit or explicit threat of financial ruin in the case of a boss in a job.
4. Adoration
The worst way to lead is through the avenue of adoration. Being liked is an effective way to get some things done some of the time.
